## Retrieval practice revision

- Five deterministic stages: open encoding, final two lexical words, explicit Arabic particle scaffold, first Arabic letter + first two Turkish letters, sequential full recall. Particle matching is a transparent finite list, not a morphological parser.
- Standard, cumulative chaining (1; 2; 1+2; 3; 1+2+3...), and three-choice retrieval. Incorrect answers reveal the source word, shake its tile and save it as difficult. Difficulty marks are browser-local; the focus filter revisits those marks. This is user-directed review, not a timed Leitner scheduling algorithm.
- Repeat uses listen → duration-matched silence (4 seconds when unavailable) → check once. Silence temporarily shows first-letter cues; cancellation owns timers, audio and speech callbacks.
- Source accuracy takes precedence over guessed word boundaries. Fatiha retains independently corrected word readings; exact source-aligned words/phrases remain separate. Where an individual Turkish reading is unavailable, the intact Arabic/Turkish source phrase appears together inside a tile. This degraded phrase granularity also affects concealment and quiz granularity. No detached orange reading strip remains. Acik Kuran was rechecked this revision and still failed DNS (ENOTFOUND).
- Research rationale: repeated retrieval improved delayed retention in Karpicke & Roediger (2008), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/ . Karpicke & Roediger (2010), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19966244/ , found no superiority of expanding over equally spaced schedules in their text experiments. These papers do not establish this app's effectiveness, a universal best first-letter method, or that random masking cannot help. No such claims are shown in the UI.
